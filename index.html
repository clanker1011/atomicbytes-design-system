02

Principles

Whimsical, still craft-serious. A toy workshop that ships — not a circus, not dark SaaS.

  • mood

    Atomic optimism

    Mid-century poster energy. Confident smirk, not corporate grin. Hope with ink under the fingernails.

  • surface

    Ink on paper

    Warm cream field, warm near-black type. Everything sits on paper — never a black void with neon fog.

  • accents

    Stickers, not glows

    Coral, mint, cobalt, yolk fill shapes that keep an ink outline. No blurry drops, no glass, no rainbow haze.

  • tone

    Craft, not circus

    Fun is allowed. Clowning is not. One accent per component; two at most. Serious studio with a wink.

03

Color

All UI sits on warm paper. Accents behave like inks, stickers, and stamps.

  • Paper #F6F1E8 --paper
  • Paper shade #EBE4D6 --paper-shade
  • Ink #1C1915 --ink
  • Muted #5A544A --muted
  • Coral #FF5B3C --coral / --accent
  • Mint #2EE6A6 --mint / --accent-3
  • Cobalt #4B6BFF --cobalt / --focus
  • Yolk #FFD84A --yolk / --accent-4
  • Surface #FFFBF3 --surface
  • Surface inset #E8E0D0 --surface-inset

Do Ink rules

  • Body text is always ink on paper or surface
  • Accent fills carry ink type and an ink stroke
  • Links: ink type, cobalt underline
  • Focus: 3px cobalt ring, 3px offset
  • One accent per component (two max)

Don’t Old habits

  • Mint or yolk as body text
  • White-on-coral (fails contrast)
  • Neon glows, glass, dark SaaS voids
  • Revive #0b0c10 / cyan / violet / pink
  • Cobalt as 16px link text color

07

Characters

Five system assets. Logo mark for identity; full-body cast for personality; geometric mark for trademark moments. Lab Tech is rejected — do not ship it.

  • 1

    AtomicByte

    atomicbyte.png · atomicbyte-mark.svg

    Primary logo atom / face mark. The smirking Bohr nucleus — three thick orbits, sticker electrons, yolk core with a face.

    Use: wordmark lockups, favicon (keep the face), hero identity, default brand presence.

  • 2

    Atom Salesman

    atom-salesman.png

    Full-body mascot. Mint tux, bow tie, pointing finger — mid-century ad energy for pitches and “say hi” moments.

    Use: contact / CTA scenes, about, campaign spots. Not the default logo.

  • 3

    Byte-Bot

    byte-bot.png

    Robot mascot. Workshop helper — builds, rebuilds, shipping systems. Friendly hardware with an atom on the chest screen.

    Use: product / craft / engineering stories. Pair with ink outlines on cream.

  • 4

    Byte Rocket

    byte-rocket.png

    Rocket mascot. Launch energy — shipping, releases, “no surprise Fridays.” Atomic exhaust optional.

    Use: launch announcements, shipping systems, blog covers about releases.

  • 5

    AtomicMark

    atomic-mark.png

    Geometric trademark atom — no face. Cobalt / mint diamond rays, coral nucleus. Legal and formal identity.

    Use: trademark, watermark, formal docs, small monograms where a face would shout.

When to use which

  • Logo mark (AtomicByte + SVG) — default identity. Header, favicon, hero. Keep the face when you shrink it.
  • Full characters (Salesman, Bot, Rocket) — personality beats. One at a time on a surface; don’t cast the whole crew in one card.
  • Trademark (AtomicMark) — geometric, no face. Seals, legal, quiet corners.
  • Rejected — Lab Tech. Do not include in the system or the live site.

09

Adoption

How to use tokens.css in a future site. This design-system folder is the source of truth before the marketing rebuild.

  1. Copy the tokens

    Bring design-system/tokens.css (or the :root block) into the real site as the single color/type/space source.

  2. Load the three families

    Sixtyfour Convergence, Fraunces (opsz + wght), and Figtree from Google Fonts. Do not reintroduce Inter.

  3. Replace utility colors

    Swap existing stylesheet colors for tokens. Delete dark-theme variables and neon accents.

  4. Rebuild components from recipes

    Buttons, cards, stamps, focus — rebuild against this page rather than tinting old glass cards cream.

  5. Place characters with intent

    AtomicByte for identity; full cast for personality; AtomicMark for trademark. Never Lab Tech.
